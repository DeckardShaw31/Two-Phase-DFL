\newcommand{\AbstractText}{For a periodic-review perishable inventory-routing system with contextual forecasts, FIFO issuing, lost sales and shelf capacity, we propose decision-focused learning (DFL) at two levels: on top of a deliver-up-to-level rule, learning context- and stock-age-dependent order-up-to levels from the realised cost of whole episodes (UL-DFL), and on top of a rolling-horizon inventory-routing model, learning its planning targets (LA-DFL). Twelve policies are compared in closed loop with identical information, from base-stock rules and the rule of Crama et al.\ (2018) to deterministic, robust and scenario-based lookahead (Jia et al., 2025). We show that the inventory recourse admits FIFO-optimal solutions once early disposal is allowed and that DFL stationary points reduce to the perishable newsvendor fractile. Stock-age-based rules with optimally routed orders are 2--12\% cheaper than every integrated policy in twelve of thirteen settings; with expensive routing, a lookahead with day-specific safety quantiles matches them. On fresh test episodes, UL-DFL is 1.1\% cheaper than its rule re-tuned on a fine grid and wastes 28\% less at nearly equal fill rate, which no parameterisation of the rule achieves. Across thirteen settings it is significantly cheaper than the rule in nine and wastes less in twelve, but on observed fresh-food demand its corrections did not transfer to new products ($+0.6$\%). Without the rule, the same learner is significantly costlier in twelve settings, even with order-up-to structure. LA-DFL improves the deterministic lookahead by up to 5\% by learning that only the implemented day needs a high safety quantile, a gain that a day-specific quantile level reproduces.}

\newcommand{\CoverResult}{On fresh test episodes of the base case, decision-focused learning on top of the deliver-up-to-level rule (UL-DFL) is 1.1\% cheaper than the rule re-tuned on a fine grid (Holm-adjusted $p<0.001$) and wastes 28\% less; across the thirteen settings it is significantly cheaper than the rule in nine, wastes less in twelve and is the cheapest policy in ten. On observed fresh-food demand the learned corrections did not transfer to new products, and the re-tuned rule was cheaper; we report this as a limitation. Decision-focused learning applied to the deterministic lookahead improves it, but a day-specific quantile level reproduces the gain, which we report as an ablation.}

\newcommand{\SensitivityText}{Five patterns emerge across the thirteen configurations, which are evaluated on the earlier test episodes (Section~\ref{sec:design}). (i) UL-DFL is significantly cheaper than the re-tuned UL$_\lambda$ in nine configurations, by 0.8--1.2\% (in eight of twelve if the clustered and mixed layouts, in which both policies place identical orders, are counted once). All three training seeds are cheaper than the rule in every configuration except high demand variability, where their signs differ, and the real data, where all three are more expensive (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). UL-DFL is the cheapest implementable policy in ten configurations; the rule itself is cheapest with high variability and on the real data, and LA-Quantile2 with expensive routing, where UL-DFL is level with it ($+0.1$\%, 95\% CI \ULciLAQTwoRouteThree{}). In twelve configurations UL-DFL wastes 7--30\% fewer units than the rule, at a fill rate within 0.6 points. The configurations without a significant gain are instructive. With a four-day shelf life the rule wastes only \wasteULMFour\% of the delivered units, so the corrections have little to remove: UL-DFL lowers the waste rate to \wasteULDFLMFour\% but saves only \ULabsrelULMFour\% in total (CI \ULciULMFour{}). With a loose fleet the saving is \ULabsrelULFleetLoose\% (CI \ULciULFleetLoose{}), and with high demand variability the waste saving (18\% fewer units) is offset by lost sales (fill rate \fillULDFLHighVar\% instead of \fillULHighVar\%). With expensive routing the gain has a different source: UL-DFL raises the order-up-to levels so that stores are visited less often (\visitsULDFLRouteThree{} instead of \visitsULRouteThree{} visits per day) and accepts 39\% more waste. On the real data UL-DFL is \ULabsrelULFresh\% more expensive than the rule (CI \ULciULFresh{}; Section~\ref{sec:realresults}). The value of learning the order-up-to level is therefore largest when perishability makes the rule's single service level costly but demand is predictable enough from context to act on it, and it requires corrections that carry over from the training data to the episodes on which the policy is used. (ii) Without learning, the stock-age-based rules are cheaper than every integrated policy in twelve configurations: the best rule is 2.0--11.9\% below the best integrated policy. The advantage is largest when lost sales are expensive ($p_u=40$, 11.9\%) and in the clustered layout (9.3\%), where all stores are close to each other, so that skipping a store saves little travel. It is smallest with a low lost-sales penalty ($p_u=10$, 2.0\%), where the high service level of the rules is worth less, and with a two-day shelf life or a loose or tight fleet (3.2--3.6\%). Partial LIFO issuing raises the waste rate of every policy, by 41--105\%, but does not change which policies are best. (iii) With three times higher routing cost, integration pays: LA-Quantile2 is the cheapest policy, 1.1\% below UL$_\lambda$ and level with UL-DFL, and daily base-stock (BSP-EW) is more expensive than every uncertainty-aware lookahead. The trigger of UL$_\lambda$, which skips stores whose stock suffices, keeps the deliver-up-to-level rules competitive. (iv) Among the integrated policies, LA-Quantile2 is best in ten configurations and ScenPO-SS in three ($m=2$, $p_u=40$ and the tight fleet). LA-Mean is the most expensive policy in all thirteen. ScenPO-SS, whose scenario set does not extend beyond the predicted 0.9 quantile, under-stocks in every configuration (fill rate 87.2--94.7\%). Sampling its scenarios from the full quantile grid instead lowers its cost in twelve configurations (significantly in nine) and would make it the cheapest policy with expensive routing, but would not change the cheapest policy elsewhere (Appendix~\ref{app:variants}). (v) LA-DFL is significantly cheaper than LA-Quantile in seven configurations (by 1.7--5.0\%), not significantly different in five and more expensive with a two-day shelf life, and it is more expensive than LA-Quantile2 in every configuration (by 0.3--5.7\%, significant in nine): on top of the lookahead, the value of decision-focused learning lies in revealing that the safety stock should depend on the planning day, which a two-parameter policy then implements.}
