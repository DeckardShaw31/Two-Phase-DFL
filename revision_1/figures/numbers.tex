\newcommand{\AbstractText}{For a periodic-review perishable inventory-routing system with contextual forecasts, FIFO issuing, lost sales and shelf capacity, we propose decision-focused learning (DFL) at two levels: on top of a deliver-up-to-level inventory rule, learning context- and stock-age-dependent order-up-to levels from the realised cost of whole episodes (UL-DFL), and on top of a rolling-horizon inventory-routing model, learning its planning targets (LA-DFL). Twelve policies are compared in closed loop with identical information, from base-stock rules and the rule of Crama et al.\ (2018) to deterministic, robust and scenario-based lookahead (Jia et al., 2025) solved to a proven 0.5\% gap. We show that the inventory recourse admits FIFO-optimal solutions once early disposal is allowed and that DFL stationary points reduce to the perishable newsvendor fractile. Across thirteen settings, including three network layouts and observed fresh-food demand, UL-DFL is cheaper than its rule in every setting, significantly in eleven (0.5--1.2\%), and 2.9--10.3\% cheaper than the best integrated policy unless routing is expensive. In the base case it serves the same fill rate with a fifth less waste, which no parameterisation of the rule achieves; a rule tuned in hindsight matches its cost but wastes 41\% more. It learns to build stock before weekly demand peaks. The same learner without the rule's structure is never cheaper than UL-DFL. Only with expensive routing is scenario-based lookahead best. LA-DFL improves the deterministic lookahead by up to 5\% by learning that only the implemented day needs a high safety quantile, a gain that a day-specific quantile level reproduces.}

\newcommand{\CoverResult}{Across the settings, decision-focused learning applied on top of the deliver-up-to-level rule (UL-DFL) is the cheapest policy in eleven of thirteen settings (tied with its rule-free ablation in a twelfth; scenario-based lookahead is cheaper only when routing is expensive), and cheaper than the rule it starts from in every setting, significantly in eleven; the learned corrections are interpretable (lower levels on promotion days and after the weekly demand peak). Decision-focused learning applied to the deterministic lookahead improves it, but its gain is reproduced by a day-specific quantile level, which we report as an ablation.}

\newcommand{\SensitivityText}{Five patterns emerge across the thirteen configurations. (i) UL-DFL is cheaper than UL$_\lambda$ in every configuration and significantly so in eleven, by 0.5--1.2\%; all three seeds improve on UL$_\lambda$ in every configuration (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). It is the cheapest implementable policy in eleven configurations, ties with NN-DFL for a two-day shelf life (difference 0.0\%, 95\% CI $[-45, 55]$), and is 2.9--10.3\% below the best integrated policy in all configurations except the one with expensive routing. Its fill rate stays within 0.7 points of that of UL$_\lambda$, and in twelve configurations it wastes 6--38\% fewer units; only with a two-day shelf life does it instead raise the fill rate (from \fillULMTwo\% to \fillULDFLMTwo\%) at somewhat more waste. The two configurations without a significant gain are instructive. With a four-day shelf life UL$_\lambda$ wastes only \wasteULMFour\% of the delivered units, so the corrections have little to remove: UL-DFL cuts waste by 38\% but gives up 0.4 points of fill rate, and the net saving is \ULabsrelULMFour\% (95\% CI \ULciULMFour{}). With high demand variability the waste saving (16\%) is largely offset by additional lost sales, a net \ULabsrelULHighVar\% (CI \ULciULHighVar{}). The value of learning the order-up-to level is therefore largest when perishability makes the rule's single service level costly but demand is predictable enough from context to act on it. (ii) Without learning, the stock-age-based rules remain cheaper than every integrated policy except with expensive routing: the best rule is 2.0--9.3\% below the best integrated policy. The advantage is largest in the clustered layout, where all stores are close to each other, so that skipping a store saves little travel, and smallest with a low lost-sales penalty ($p_u=10$), where the high service level of the rules is worth less, and with a loose or tight fleet (2.0--2.9\%). Partial LIFO issuing raises the waste rate of every policy, by 40--90\%, but does not change which policies are best. (iii) With three times higher routing cost, integration pays: ScenPO-SS is the cheapest policy, 2.7\% below UL-DFL and 3.5\% below UL$_\lambda$, and daily base-stock (BSP-EW) becomes more expensive than every uncertainty-aware lookahead. UL-DFL is still the cheapest rule, because the trigger of UL$_\lambda$ already skips stores whose stock suffices. (iv) Among the integrated policies, LA-Quantile2 is best in eight configurations and ScenPO-SS in the other five: when lost sales are expensive ($p_u=40$), the shelf life is two days, the fleet is tight, routing is expensive, and on the real data. LA-Mean is the most expensive policy in all thirteen. (v) LA-DFL is significantly cheaper than LA-Quantile in seven configurations (by 1.7--5.0\%), not significantly different in five and more expensive with a two-day shelf life, and it is more expensive than LA-Quantile2 in every configuration (by 0.3--5.7\%, significant in nine): on top of the lookahead, the value of decision-focused learning lies in revealing that the safety stock should depend on the planning day, which a two-parameter policy then implements.}
