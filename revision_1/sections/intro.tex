\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ============================================================================

Fresh food is replenished daily, its stock ages every night, and whatever is not sold within its shelf life is thrown away. Retailers and their distributors therefore face the classical perishable inventory trade-off between waste and lost sales \citep{nahmias1982perishable,bakker2012review}, but with two complications that single-store inventory models do not capture. First, deliveries to different stores share a small fleet: a store that is not on today's route cannot be replenished today, and a vehicle that is full cannot top up the next store. Second, retailers now have rich contextual information---promotion calendars, weather forecasts, recent sales---that machine-learning forecasters turn into store-day demand predictions. The operational question is how such forecasts should be translated into replenishment and delivery decisions when stock ages, shelf space is limited and vehicles are shared.

Two literatures answer different halves of this question. Perishable inventory control offers simple, robust ordering rules---base-stock policies \citep{bu2023asymptotic}, and rules that anticipate the waste of the current stock \citep{broekmeulen2009heuristic,haijema2019improved}---but treats each store in isolation. Inventory routing integrates replenishment and routing, with exact methods for deterministic perishable demand \citep{coelho2014optimal,alvarez2020formulations}, decomposition and order-up-to rules for stochastic perishable demand \citep{crama2018stochastic}, robust formulations \citep{solyali2012robust,he2024robust} and, recently, scenario-based predict-then-optimise policies that use machine-learning quantile forecasts \citep{jia2025scenario}. In parallel, decision-focused learning (DFL) trains forecasters on the cost of the decisions they induce rather than on forecast accuracy \citep{elmachtoub2022smart,mandi2024decision}. Whether DFL adds anything once the strong inventory rules and stochastic lookahead methods are on the table has not been tested for perishable inventory routing.

This paper studies a periodic-review perishable inventory-routing system with contextual demand, lost sales, FIFO issuing and shelf capacity, and compares nine replenishment policies in closed loop, every day observing the age-structured stock and the same forecasts. The comparison includes the inventory-control rules a perishable-inventory manager would use (base-stock with and without estimated waste), the deliver-up-to-level rule of \citet{crama2018stochastic}, and rolling-horizon inventory-routing models driven by mean, quantile, robust and scenario forecasts, the last being the Scenario Predict-then-Optimize method of \citet{jia2025scenario}. We propose a decision-focused policy, LA-DFL, that keeps a deterministic rolling-horizon plan but learns how far each planning target should deviate from the forecast, depending on context and on the age of the store's stock. The learning signal is the realised FIFO cost of the deliveries the plan induces, differentiated through a coverage layer while an exact MILP oracle fixes the routes.

The answer is more nuanced than either literature suggests. Across ten settings, daily base-stock rules that use the age of the stock, with their orders routed optimally, were the cheapest policies in nine; only when travel was expensive was the scenario-based lookahead best. Decision-focused learning improved the deterministic lookahead in nine settings and made it the best integrated policy in five, largely by learning that only the executed day needs a high safety quantile, but it did not overturn the advantage of the inventory rules.

The paper makes three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{An inventory-centred benchmark of replenishment policies under routing coupling.} We evaluate stock-age-based inventory rules, a published stochastic PIRP rule, and integrated robust, scenario-based and decision-focused lookahead policies under one closed-loop simulator, with every lookahead solved by an exact MILP to a proven gap. Outcomes are reported in inventory terms---cost composition, fill rate, waste rate, delivery frequency and store-level service---across shelf life, cost ratio, fleet tightness, demand variability and issuing policy.
\item \textbf{A decision-focused policy grounded in perishable inventory structure.} We show that the inventory recourse admits FIFO-optimal solutions once early disposal is allowed, bound the marginal value of a delivery by the lost-sales and waste penalties, and prove that stationary points of the decision-focused objective satisfy the stochastic optimality conditions of the fixed-route problem, reducing to the perishable newsvendor fractile for one-day shelf life. These results also correct claims of an earlier version of this work, and all of them are verified numerically.
\item \textbf{Managerial evidence on when integration, stochastic lookahead and learning pay off.} The results show when simple stock-age-based rules suffice and when integration with routing pays, why deterministic plans over-stock, and how integrated plans shift stock-outs to peripheral stores, across shelf life, penalty ratio, fleet capacity, routing cost, demand variability and issuing policy (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}
