\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Managerial implications}

The study asked how contextual forecasts should be turned into replenishment decisions when perishable stock ages and deliveries share vehicles. Five implications follow for planners of fresh-food distribution.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Benchmark against simple stock-age-based rules before integrating.} Daily base-stock replenishment at a high service quantile, with the resulting orders routed optimally, was the cheapest policy in nine of the ten settings, by 2.4--8.9\% over the best integrated policy, including longer and shorter shelf lives, lower and higher lost-sales penalties, tighter and looser fleets, higher demand variability and partial LIFO issuing. In these settings the travel saved by consolidating deliveries is smaller than the extra holding, waste and lost sales that consolidation causes. This extends the finding of \citet{haijema2019improved} and \citet{crama2018stochastic} that well-tuned simple rules are hard to beat to a setting with shared routes and contextual forecasts.
\item \textbf{Integration pays when routing is expensive, and then uncertainty must be modelled explicitly.} With three times higher travel cost, the scenario-based lookahead of \citet{jia2025scenario} was the cheapest policy and every uncertainty-aware lookahead beat daily base-stock. Deterministic lookahead with mean forecasts was the worst policy in every setting; quantile targets repair much of this. Among the uncertainty-aware lookahead methods none dominated: the scenario lookahead was best in four settings, including those with tight capacity or expensive routing, but the weakest of them when the fleet had ample slack.
\item \textbf{If a deterministic plan is used, its safety stock should depend on the planning day.} The decision-focused correction learned to keep a high service quantile only for the day that is actually executed and to plan later days near the 0.76--0.79 quantile. A planner can implement this insight without machine learning by tuning day-specific quantile levels; it removed about half of the waste of the deterministic quantile plan in the base case.
\item \textbf{Look at store-level service, not only at averages.} Integrated plans concentrated stock-outs at stores that are expensive to reach, whereas the inventory rules served all stores alike. Retailers with per-store service targets should constrain or monitor this effect.
\item \textbf{Decision-focused learning is a refinement, not a substitute for good inventory structure.} DFL never made the deterministic lookahead significantly worse, improved it in nine of ten settings (significantly in five) and was the cheapest integrated policy in five, lowering stock where the quantile plan over-stocked and raising it where it under-stocked. It did not, however, beat the best inventory rule in any setting. Its value lies in correcting a fast deterministic planner at no extra online cost, not in replacing stochastic modelling or well-tuned replenishment rules.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and further research}

The demand data are generated by a documented contextual model rather than observed at a retailer, so cost levels should not be read as estimates for a particular chain; testing the policies on store-level sales of fresh products, including censored demand, is the most important next step. Exact routing by enumeration limits the network to about a dozen stores; larger networks require heuristic routing, which would weaken the clean separation between replenishment and routing quality used here. Lead time is zero and each store sells one product; positive lead times would increase the value of stock-age information \citep{broekmeulen2009heuristic}, and multiple products would couple stores through shared vehicle space. The lookahead policies relax routing integrality beyond the implemented day, which favours visiting stores more often than a fully integer lookahead would. LA-DFL was trained on the decision states visited by LA-Quantile in one round; iterating between training and data collection, learning visit decisions as well, or training the scenario generator of ScenPO-SS decision-focusedly are natural extensions. Finally, deep reinforcement learning has shown promise for single-location perishable inventory \citep{demoor2022reward,temizoz2025deep}; extending it to the routing-coupled action space studied here is an open problem.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Replenishing perishables on shared routes is a perishable inventory problem first and a routing problem second. Under a common closed-loop simulator with exact optimisation and ten settings, stock-age-based base-stock rules were the strongest policies unless travel was expensive, in which case scenario-based stochastic lookahead was best; decision-focused learning made a fast deterministic lookahead competitive with stochastic and robust lookahead and revealed why deterministic plans mis-stock, but it did not overturn these conclusions. The structural results, the experimental protocol and the code are available for testing further policies on the same benchmark.
