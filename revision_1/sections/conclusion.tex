\section{Discussion and conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Managerial implications}

The study asked how contextual forecasts should be turned into replenishment decisions when perishable stock ages and deliveries share vehicles. Five implications follow for planners of fresh-food distribution.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Start from a stock-age-based replenishment rule and benchmark against it before integrating.} Daily replenishment at a high service quantile, with the orders routed optimally, was cheaper than every integrated inventory-routing policy in all settings but the one with expensive routing, by 0.9--9.3\%, across three network layouts, shelf lives of two to four days, lower and higher lost-sales penalties, tighter and looser fleets, more variable demand, partial LIFO issuing and observed demand of a fresh-food retailer; the margin was smallest when lost sales were cheap, so that high service is worth less. In these settings the travel saved by consolidating deliveries is smaller than the extra holding, waste and lost sales that consolidation causes. This extends the finding of \citet{haijema2019improved} and \citet{crama2018stochastic} that well-tuned simple rules are hard to beat to a setting with shared routes and contextual forecasts.
\item \textbf{Learn the order-up-to level, not only the forecast.} Training the order-up-to level of the deliver-up-to-level rule on the realised cost of its decisions reduced cost in every setting, significantly in eleven of thirteen, and in the base case it reached the same fill rate with a fifth less waste, which no re-tuning of the rule's service level and trigger achieves; a rule tuned with hindsight matched its cost only by wasting 41\% more. On the real data the gain came mainly from fewer weekend stock-outs. The gain was smallest, and not significant, where the rule already wasted little (four-day shelf life) or where demand was too variable for the context to anticipate it, so the learning effort pays most for short-lived products with predictable demand patterns. It also needs the rule: the same network trained to order directly was more expensive than UL-DFL in twelve settings and than the plain rule in eleven. The policy is operated exactly like the rule: it remains a rule with a visit trigger and an order-up-to level that planners can inspect. The learned corrections translate into practice that does not require machine learning: shift safety stock from the days after the weekly peak to the days before it, and, where promotions widen the forecast spread, lower the order-up-to level on promotion days.
\item \textbf{Integration pays when routing is expensive, and then uncertainty must be modelled explicitly.} With three times higher travel cost, the scenario-based lookahead of \citet{jia2025scenario} was the cheapest policy and every uncertainty-aware lookahead beat daily base-stock. Deterministic lookahead with mean forecasts was the worst policy in every setting.
\item \textbf{If a deterministic plan is used, its safety stock should depend on the planning day.} Decision-focused learning on top of the lookahead learned to keep a high service quantile only for the day that is actually executed and to plan later days near the 0.8 quantile. A lookahead with these two quantile levels, tuned on validation data, removes about half of the waste of the single-quantile plan in the base case and was at least as cheap as the learned targets in all thirteen settings.
\item \textbf{Look at store-level service, not only at averages.} Integrated deterministic plans concentrated stock-outs at individual stores, whereas the inventory rules served all stores alike. Retailers with per-store service targets should constrain or monitor this effect.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations and further research}

The synthetic demand is generated by a documented contextual model; the real-data case uses observed sales of one retailer, from which latent demand had to be reconstructed under stock-outs and rescaled to the network's capacities, and from which products whose level shifted strongly were excluded. Exact routing by enumeration limits the network to about a dozen stores; larger networks require heuristic routing, which would weaken the clean separation between replenishment and routing quality used here. Lead time is zero and each store sells one product; positive lead times would increase the value of stock-age information \citep{broekmeulen2009heuristic}, and multiple products would couple stores through shared vehicle space. The lookahead policies relax routing integrality beyond the implemented day, which favours visiting stores more often than a fully integer lookahead would. UL-DFL learns the order-up-to level but keeps the rule's visit trigger and sees fleet capacity only as a proportional scaling during training; learning the trigger as well, or training against the exact routing cost, could extend its advantage to settings with expensive routing, where it was not the best policy. LA-DFL was trained on the decision states visited by LA-Quantile in one round; iterating between training and data collection, or training the scenario generator of ScenPO-SS decision-focusedly, are natural extensions. Finally, deep reinforcement learning has shown promise for single-location perishable inventory \citep{demoor2022reward,temizoz2025deep}; extending it to the routing-coupled action space studied here is an open problem.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Replenishing perishables on shared routes is a perishable inventory problem first and a routing problem second. Under a common closed-loop simulator with exact optimisation, stock-age-based replenishment rules were the strongest policies without learning unless travel was expensive, in which case scenario-based stochastic lookahead was best. Decision-focused learning made the best rule cheaper in every setting, significantly in eleven of thirteen, by learning from realised cost when to deviate from a fixed service quantile; on top of a deterministic lookahead it revealed why such plans mis-stock, a lesson that a day-specific quantile level implements without learning. The structural results, the experimental protocol and the code are available for testing further policies on the same benchmark.
