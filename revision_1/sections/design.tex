\section{Experimental design}\label{sec:design}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Network, products and costs}

The network consists of a depot and the first ten customers of Solomon's R101 instance \citep{solomon1987algorithms}; coordinates define Euclidean distances and time windows are ignored. Two vehicles serve the stores. Vehicle capacity is set through the fleet ratio $\rho=KQ/\bar D$, where $\bar D$ is mean total daily demand; the base case has $\rho=1.25$ (\VehicleCap{} units per vehicle), so that the fleet can carry a quarter more than average demand, as is common in grocery distribution where vehicles are sized for peak days. Shelf capacity is three days of mean store demand. The base product has a shelf life of $m=3$ days. Costs are $h=1$ per unit and night, $p_s=5$ per expired unit and $p_u=20$ per lost sale, with routing cost one per distance unit; per unit of demand, waste is five times and a lost sale twenty times as expensive as one night of holding, the asymmetry studied in the original submission.

\subsection{Demand and information}

Demand is generated by a contextual negative-binomial model (code in the replication package). Store base levels range from 10 to 22 units per day; demand varies with the day of the week (Saturday $+30\%$), announced promotions (12\% of store-days, discounts of 10--30\%, multiplying demand by up to 1.8 and lowering the negative-binomial size parameter from 8 to 3.2, so that promotions raise both level and variability), temperature, holidays, and a latent store level following a log-AR(1) process with autocorrelation 0.92 that can only be inferred from recent sales. The coefficient of variation of daily store demand is about 0.4 on regular days. The temperature forecast error grows with the lead time. The demand model is hand-specified; it is designed to reproduce qualitative features of fresh retail demand (weekly pattern, promotion lifts, heteroscedasticity, persistence), not calibrated to a specific retailer, and no results should be read as estimates for a particular chain.

Each experiment uses 100 training, 20 validation and 40 test episodes, generated with disjoint random seeds. An episode consists of 35 days of history (to compute lagged features), 21 decision days, and four further days so that lookahead forecasts and the perfect-information reference are defined on the last decision days. Every policy starts each test episode with empty stores and is evaluated on the same 40 demand paths (common random numbers).

\subsection{Configurations}

Besides the base case we vary one factor at a time: shelf life $m\in\{2,4\}$; lost-sales penalty $p_u\in\{10,40\}$; fleet ratio $\rho\in\{1.10,1.60\}$; routing cost $c^{\mathrm{r}}=3$ per distance unit, which makes consolidation of deliveries more valuable; demand variability (negative-binomial size 3 instead of 8, i.e.\ coefficient of variation about 0.6 on regular days); and partial LIFO issuing, where 30\% of customers take the freshest units. In the LIFO configuration all policies are tuned and trained under FIFO and evaluated under mixed issuing, which tests robustness to a misspecified issuing assumption that practitioners often face.

\subsection{Tuning, training and inference}

All parameters are selected on validation episodes by closed-loop simulation: BSP and BSP-EW over $R\in\{1,2\}$ and $\alpha\in\{0.6,0.7,0.8,0.9,0.95\}$; UL$_\lambda$ over $\lambda\in\{1,2,3\}$, $\alpha\in\{0.7,0.8,0.9,0.95\}$, $\beta\in\{0.5,0.7,0.9\}$; LA-Quantile over $\alpha\in\{0.5,\dots,0.9\}$; LA-Robust over $\Gamma\in\{0.25,0.5,1,1.5\}$. LA-DFL is trained on the decision states visited by the tuned LA-Quantile policy in 60 training episodes (1,260 states), with eight epochs, visit patterns re-optimised every second epoch, Adam (learning rate $2\cdot10^{-3}$, minibatch 64), a two-layer network with 64 SiLU units, and validation-based model selection. Test episodes are used once.

The prespecified primary hypotheses compare LA-DFL with LA-Quantile, ScenPO-SS, LA-Robust, BSP-EW and UL$_\lambda$ in the base case. The unit of analysis is the episode; for each comparison we report the mean paired cost difference, a 95\% paired bootstrap interval (10,000 resamples), paired $t$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Holm-adjusted $p$-values over the five primary comparisons \citep{holm1979simple}. All other comparisons and configurations are reported with paired intervals as secondary evidence.

\subsection{Optimisation settings}\label{sec:solver}

Lookahead MILPs are solved by HiGHS through SciPy with a relative gap of 0.5\% and time limits of 20 seconds (deterministic) and 60 seconds (scenario), with integer vehicle assignments on the implemented day and relaxed assignments on later lookahead days (Section~\ref{sec:lookahead}). The DFL oracle uses the same model with a 1\% gap. If a time limit were reached the best incumbent would be used and its proven gap recorded; Section~\ref{sec:computation} reports solver statistics. All experiments run on a 16-core desktop computer with 15 parallel worker processes; the code, seeds and configuration files are in the replication package.
