## 1. Decision and audit result

The previous Phase 2 draft was **not citation-safe**. Most named researchers and several underlying papers are real, but several bibliographic records, publication years, methods, or claims were wrong. One arXiv identifier pointed to an unrelated reinforcement-learning paper. This revision corrects the known records and attributions, but it does not certify every row in the full matrix; the old “six tiers are state of the art” and “Gate 2 compliance verified” language must not be used.

The editor’s critique also cannot be answered by adding recent citations alone. Relevant stochastic perishable inventory-routing research already exists, including Crama et al. (2018) and He et al. (2024). The revised positioning below acknowledges that work, distinguishes direct PIRP studies from adjacent inventory, routing, control, and DFL studies, and narrows the candidate gap to the particular combination studied here. It does not claim that the proposed comparators have been run.

## 4. Revised operational positioning

The literature already studies fixed-life inventory policies, deterministic perishable IRPs, stochastic perishable routing, robust uncertain-demand PIRP, and contextual online IRP. The manuscript must therefore avoid claims such as “no prior study considers perishability with demand uncertainty” or “DFL has never been applied to inventory routing.”

The defensible candidate contribution is narrower: examine a hand-specified synthetic demand benchmark in which contextual forecasts feed a multi-period, fixed-shelf-life, FIFO inventory-routing model with a shared capacitated fleet and asymmetric holding, spoilage, and shortage costs; then test whether aligning prediction with this model changes realized service, waste, carryover, routing, and cost relative to operational policies. No M5 observations are ingested and no empirical calibration has been established. The distinction from Crama et al. and He et al. must be made by comparing their exact assumptions, objectives, information timing, solution methods, and evaluation settings against this study—not by claiming the combination is unprecedented based on a keyword search.

The current results are not field validation. Demand, weather, promotion, and price effects are all generated by a hand-specified process. Reported DFL performance cannot be described as state of the art.

## 5. Comparator shortlist for Phase 3

The corrected primary experiment evaluates a short, source-linked set. A separate exploratory finite-scenario robust adaptation was added after the primary pilot results had been inspected; it is not part of the frozen primary comparison family.

1. **Crama et al. (2018) UL_lambda policy:** The current primary experiment includes an open-loop validation-residual adaptation of the paper's deliver-up-to-level rule. The source policy re-evaluates inventory after actual demand in each period, then applies a current-period service threshold and an up-to-level target for the next `lambda` periods. The workspace implementation instead propagates predicted inventory across a fixed five-period plan and uses a route-packing heuristic. It is explicitly not a faithful replication and is not an information-matched comparison with the source policy.
2. **Point-forecast PIRP:** LightGBM point predictions followed by the same age-structured PIRP optimizer used for the other forecast-based method; this supplies the predict-then-optimize operational reference.
3. **Asymmetric quantile PTO:** LightGBM quantile forecasts at the stated inventory-cost critical fractile, followed by the same PIRP optimizer. This is a forecasting/decision comparator rather than a published perishable IRP policy.
4. **Two-Phase DFL:** Retained as the method under evaluation, not presumed to be best.
5. **Empirical robust finite-scenario plan selector (custom adaptation):** Generates five deterministic plans from validation residual paths at aggregate-residual quantiles, evaluates each fixed plan against the complete validation residual scenario bank using the common FIFO cost, and selects the lowest worst-case-cost candidate. It uses the same episode-start information and is evaluated on the same 60 test episodes. It is not He et al.'s RCR, BD, or ILS-BD and was added post hoc.

The prior code's “rolling horizon,” “SAA,” “ALNS,” and “PIL” labels are withdrawn. Those implementations are custom exploratory routines, not source-faithful implementations, and are excluded from primary evidence. The new finite-scenario policy is a restricted plan-selection heuristic; it does not solve a robust extensive form or reproduce the direct robust source method. A full-information heuristic plan is not used to compute primary regret or a claimed lower bound.

Crama et al.'s primary source confirms the expected-value and UL_lambda rule structure. The coded comparator is a disclosed adaptation, so its results support only the value of this specific policy implementation, not a claim to reproduce the paper's dynamic UL_lambda, SDP, or decomposition algorithms.

### Contemporary comparator required before resubmission

He et al. (2024) should remain the first source-method comparator considered for closing the editor's operational-comparison concern because it is a recent direct robust perishable-PIRP study. The official journal record verifies its robust-counterpart reformulation, Benders decomposition, and ILS-BD; the full detailed formulation was not available in the current environment. The empirical finite-scenario adaptation above is motivated by the same problem class but does not implement those methods or He et al.'s stated uncertainty set. Jia, Schrotenboer, and Chen (2025), *Scenario Predict-then-Optimize for Data-Driven Online Inventory Routing*, is a real, recent *Transportation Science* paper (59(5), 1032–1056; DOI 10.1287/trsc.2024.0613). Its full paper describes online inventory routing, multi-horizon quantile-RNN forecasts, sampled demand scenarios, and scenario-based stochastic optimization. The formulation reviewed has no finite-life age-cohort state; adding FIFO age tracking and expiry would therefore be an adaptation. Its online policy also requires a common online decision-information protocol. Neither source method has been implemented. The benchmark therefore does not establish state-of-the-art performance.

## 6. Frozen internal comparison protocol (revision2)

This local protocol was frozen before the revised method outcomes were computed. It is not an external preregistration. Deviations must be recorded in `results/revision2_final/run_manifest.json`.

- **Primary setting:** C1 network; five periods; shelf life `tau=3`; holding cost `h=1`; expiry/disposal penalty `p_s=5`; unmet-demand penalty `p_u=20`; two vehicles of capacity `Q=100`; store capacity `C_i=70`; zero initial inventory at the start of each independent episode. Inventory carries over by age within an episode.
- **Information set:** one five-period plan is made at episode start. Calendar, announced promotion, discount, price, store identity, and pre-episode demand summaries are available. Realized future demand and realized future weather are excluded. No method observes test demand before the full plan is fixed. All methods are evaluated on the same held-out paths using the FIFO simulator.
- **Data split:** 400 independently seeded synthetic demand episodes (master seed `20261028`), split 280/60/60 into train/validation/test under `data/revision2_final/processed/`. The 60 test episodes are the inference units and were generated with a seed distinct from the excluded smoke run. A normal-approximation design targets 80% power for a paired standardized effect of 0.45 with two-sided alpha 0.025 for the two primary cost comparisons; this is a design assumption, not a guarantee of achieved power.
- **Methods:** LightGBM point PTO; LightGBM quantile PTO at `p_u/(p_u+h+p_s/tau)`; a Crama et al. (2018) UL_lambda-inspired adaptation; and an approximate two-phase DFL hybrid. The UL_lambda adaptation tunes `lambda in {1,2,3}` and target service level in `{0.80,0.90,0.95,0.98}` on validation cost using LightGBM residuals. It uses a shared nearest-neighbor/2-opt route-packing heuristic and open-loop predicted inventory propagation; it is not a faithful reproduction of Crama et al.'s dynamic policy or stronger methods.
- **Training:** LightGBM settings are fixed at 100 trees, depth 5, learning rate 0.05. DFL uses 50 epochs, batch size 16, transition ratio 0.8, best validation checkpoint, and seeds 101/202/303. In the 50-epoch fast path, most minibatches use a vectorized capacity-projected polyhedral surrogate; selected minibatches in the final integer phase request the configured MILP oracle, which can fall back to a feasible construction. This is not a full-MILP-per-batch SPO+ implementation. Seeds quantify optimization variability and do not increase the number of independent test episodes.
- **Optimization:** forecast-based PIRP plans and DFL training request SciPy/HiGHS MILP with `mip_rel_gap=0.01` and a 0.5-second time limit. The LP-guided construction heuristic is a feasibility-checked fallback; optimality is never assumed. The UL_lambda adaptation uses the stated nearest-neighbor/2-opt route-packing heuristic for its policy targets.
- **Outcomes:** total episode cost is primary. Secondary quantities include routing, holding, expiry and capacity-disposal costs, expired/discarded units, unmet demand, fill rate, deliveries, route distance, and average/ending on-hand inventory. Expiry and capacity disposal are reported separately.
- **Inference:** compare DFL to LightGBM PTO and the UL_lambda adaptation using paired differences in episode cost, 10,000 paired episode bootstrap replicates, and Holm adjustment over the two primary comparisons. Quantile PTO and physical outcomes are secondary/descriptive. Training seeds are averaged within episode before inference.
- **Reproducibility:** preserve old `results/` artifacts as legacy. Final data and outputs go to `data/revision2_final/processed/` and `results/revision2_final/`; exploratory smoke outputs are separate and excluded. Record solver diagnostics, Python/library versions, random seeds, and input checksums.

## 7. Gate status

- **Core literature source audit:** principal Crama, Jia, and He comparator identities/method summaries were checked against primary sources; known false records and attribution errors were corrected. Other matrix entries and all manuscript claims still need a final source-by-source submission audit.
- **Bibliography/cross-reference check:** the local citation audit passed for 60 BibTeX entries and all 12 citations in `main.tex` (`results/citation_audit_revision2.json`). This checks citation resolution and DOI/permanent-URL presence; it is not a substitute for auditing the factual claim attached to each citation.
- **Literature positioning:** revised to acknowledge direct stochastic/robust perishable PIRP research and to separate adjacent work.
- **Comparator selection:** the primary run includes a qualified UL_lambda adaptation, point-forecast PIRP, quantile PTO, and approximate DFL. DFL did not show a statistically detectable cost reduction versus point PTO. Its lower cost versus the UL_lambda adaptation is specific to the open-loop heuristic implementation, not Crama et al.'s source method. The separate finite-scenario robust adaptation is custom and post hoc; the contemporary Jia scenario-PTO method and source-faithful robust perishable PIRP methods remain unimplemented, so the state-of-the-art comparison gate remains open. SAA/ALNS/MPC claims in the old module have been withdrawn.
- **Exploratory robust adaptation:** `results/revision2_robust_adaptation_exploratory/` contains a second run on all 60 held-out test episodes. Mean cost is 2712.3 (SD 1148.9), mean fill rate 0.904, and maximum candidate-plan/FIFO state error is zero. Its exploratory paired cost differences are -25.8 versus point PTO (95% bootstrap interval [-85.2, 32.7]) and -82.7 versus DFL ([-150.0, -14.9]). All 300 candidate MILP requests used the feasible construction fallback. This is not a source-faithful robust comparator and is not confirmatory evidence.
- **Protocol status:** internally frozen before the revised test benchmark; not externally preregistered.
- **Information symmetry and evaluator:** the primary pilot and robust adaptation use episode-start-known features, shared held-out paths, FIFO evaluation, and zero maximum planning-demand/FIFO state discrepancy.
